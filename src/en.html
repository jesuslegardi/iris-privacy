Privacy Policy

Iris · 1 October 2026

1What this covers

This Policy explains what personal data the Iris application collects, why it is collected, who receives it, how long it is kept, and what you can require of us. It applies to Iris for iPhone and Iris for Android, and to the server that supports them. It applies to nothing else.

“Iris” means the application and its supporting services. “We”, “us” and “our” mean the person identified at the end of this Policy.

2Signing in

You can start Iris as a guest without supplying an email address. Firebase creates an anonymous account identifier so your setup, habits, goals and optional coaching belong to you. Guest does not mean that data stays offline. You can continue with the free tracker. Saving your account with Apple or Google is optional.

  • From Apple we receive an identity token, your name and your email address. Apple sends the name and address only the first time you authorise Iris. If you chose “Hide My Email”, the address we receive is an Apple relay address and not your own. Sign in with Apple works on Android as well as on iPhone.
  • From Google we receive an identity token, your email address and the web address of your Google profile picture.

Saving a guest account with an unused Apple or Google sign-in links that sign-in to the same Iris identifier and keeps your guest data. If that sign-in already belongs to an Iris account, Iris opens that existing account; it does not merge the two accounts or their private data. Use your original sign-in method to recover an existing account. Without a saved sign-in, access to guest data can be lost if the app or its credentials are removed.

3What we collect

Your account

A Firebase account identifier, including for guests; and, when you save an account, the sign-in identifier and email address supplied by Apple or Google. The email is held in Firebase Authentication and is not part of your public profile.

Your profile

A username, which you choose; a display name; an optional short biography; an optional profile picture; and the date the account was created. Section 4 explains who can see these.

Your habits and your record

The names of the habits you track; whether each is one you are building or one you are breaking; the days and times you schedule them for; the priority and colour you give them; any description you write against a habit; and whether you marked each one done or missed. Completed weeks are archived so that your history and your consistency figures survive. Points are calculated by the application from that record — you do not supply them.

Your conversations with Iris

What you type to her. If you speak to her, what you said after your own phone has turned it into text — section 7 explains what happens to the audio itself. Her replies. Any goal she records for you, together with the words you used when you set it.

Your device

An installation identifier issued by Firebase, which is the address notifications are sent to; the application version and build; your time zone; the language you have chosen in the application; whether you have permitted notifications; the platform; and when the device was last seen. There is one such record for each device you sign in on, and it is deleted when you sign out of that device.

Your purchases

Your Iris account identifier, store product and transaction identifiers, receipt or purchase-token information, purchase history and subscription status. Apple or Google processes payment; Iris never receives your card number.

Iris does not sell personal data, show ads, or track you across other apps and websites. We record limited onboarding diagnostics under your account: steps such as starting, choosing a habit or goal, viewing an offer, continuing free, purchasing or saving an account, with timestamps and whether the account is a guest. These records contain no chat text and are removed with the account. RevenueCat subscription information and server security logs are separate. There is no third-party advertising or crash-reporting SDK. We keep at most 200 of these onboarding event records per account.

4What other people can see

Your profile record can be read by any signed-in Iris account. This is not an oversight — it is what allows another person to find you by username and send you a friend request. That record holds your username, display name, biography, the address of your profile picture, the date you joined, and your visibility settings.

The application gives you three settings — for your name, your biography and your profile picture — each with the choices Everyone, Friends and No one. Both applications obey all three wherever a person is shown, and our server obeys the name setting when it composes the text of a notification. The picture setting is enforced additionally at the storage layer, so a picture set to No one cannot be retrieved at all. The name and biography settings govern how the applications present those two fields, which sit on the record described above.

Friends. Sending a friend request writes your username, display name and picture address into the other person’s account and sends a notification to their device. Your own list of friends can be read by you alone. Blocking someone removes you from each other’s friend list; neither application tells them, though the record of the block is held in their account.

Competitions — please read this. A competition is optional and you enter one deliberately. When you do, every other accepted participant can see the names of the habits you entered and your day-by-day record of them — which you completed and which you missed, for each day of the round — together with your points and how many rounds you have won. If you also choose to share a photograph in a competition, those same participants can see it. Nothing else of yours becomes visible.

Other Iris users cannot read your habit lists, habit descriptions, weekly history, goals, milestones, settings, notification records, friend list or conversations with Iris, apart from the competition information you choose to share as explained above. If you report selected content to us, the developer can read that content to review the report.

5Photographs

Habit tracking and unlimited full-quality local habit photographs are free. Taking a photograph does not subscribe you to coaching. Only a copy you explicitly share in a competition is uploaded for the other participants.

There are four kinds, and two of them never leave your phone.

Habit evidence photographs stay on your device. They are taken with the camera inside the application; there is no way to import one from your photo library, permanently and by design. The image is written to the application’s own private storage on the phone. What reaches our database is a local reference — a short piece of text, not an image — so that your other device knows a photograph exists for that day. We never receive the image.

Milestone photographs stay on your device in the same way. These you may choose from your photo library.

Your profile picture is uploaded to our storage, and is served to other people according to the setting described in section 4.

Competition photographs are uploaded. A separate, smaller copy is made for the purpose; the full-quality original stays on your phone. The copy can be seen by the accepted participants of that competition, it is deleted when the round is decided, and it is in no case kept for longer than eight days.

After every photograph the application asks whether to keep it in Iris only, or in Iris and your phone’s gallery. If you choose the gallery, a copy is placed in your own camera roll, outside the application, where any photo synchronisation you use will treat it like any other picture.

Backups differ between the platforms, and you should know which applies to you. On iPhone, photographs held by Iris are included in your iCloud device backup. On Android they are excluded from cloud backup and are carried only in a direct phone-to-phone transfer on Android 12 and later; on earlier versions of Android they are not carried at all.

6Iris, and what reaches the companies that run the models

Iris is an artificial-intelligence coach. Her replies are generated by large language models operated by the companies named in section 8. They are not written by a person and no person reads them before you do.

For each message, our server assembles and sends: your display name; the conversation so far in the current session; a calculated summary of your tracking — habit names, which list each belongs to, priority, the days they are scheduled for, how many you have completed, missed and still have to do this week, your consistency across the last several recorded weeks, your streaks, your fuel level and your local date; your goals, in the words you used; and one habit description, and only when your message names exactly one habit unambiguously.

We do not send your email address, your photographs, any file, or any other person’s data.

Before any of it leaves our server, a fixed check reads your message in all ten languages. A message it matches is not forwarded to any company and is not recorded in your conversation; you receive a reply about getting help from a person instead.

Iris is not a medical, psychological or crisis service. She does not diagnose, treat or advise on any health condition. If you are in danger, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line.

7Voice, and the difference between the two platforms

When you speak to Iris, your phone turns your speech into text, and only that text is sent to us. The audio is never written to a file by the application, never uploaded by the application, and never kept. A single utterance is limited to sixty seconds.

On iPhone, transcription is performed on the device, always. There is no cloud fallback: where on-device transcription is not available for a language, the feature reports itself unavailable rather than sending your audio anywhere.

On Android, transcription is performed on the device when your phone is able to do it, which requires Android 13 or later and an installed model for the language. Where it cannot, your phone’s own speech service performs the transcription, and your audio is sent to that service — in practice Google’s, though it is whichever service your phone is configured to use. That transfer is governed by that service’s terms and not by this Policy. The application tells you which of the two is in use when you open voice mode, and it can change during a session if no model exists for a language you switch to.

Iris’s spoken voice is produced by Google Cloud Text-to-Speech from the text of her own reply, which our server reads from its own copy of the conversation. Your words are not sent to that service. The resulting audio is cached by us, addressed by a fingerprint of the text rather than by any person, and deleted after thirty days.

8Who receives your data

Six companies. Each acts on our instructions, for the purpose named.

Google LLC

The largest recipient, in several distinct roles: Firebase Authentication, which holds your sign-in and your email address; Cloud Firestore, the database holding everything in section 3 except your email address; Cloud Storage, holding your profile picture, competition photographs and the cached speech audio; Cloud Functions in the United States, which is our server; Firebase Cloud Messaging, which delivers notifications; Firebase App Check and Firebase Installations, for device attestation and the installation identifier; Google Sign-In, if you use it; the Gemini API, which receives your spoken conversation as described in section 6; Google Cloud Text-to-Speech, which receives Iris’s replies only; and, on Android, Google Play services for purchase infrastructure, integrity attestation and the review prompt. If you signed in with Google and have not replaced your picture, other people’s phones fetch that picture from Google’s servers directly.

OpenAI, L.L.C.

Your typed conversation and the summary described in section 6. OpenAI is the primary provider for most of what you type.

Anthropic PBC

The same material as OpenAI, for the parts of the conversation it handles and whenever OpenAI is unavailable. Separately, a list of your habit names alone is sent to Anthropic in order to sort habits into categories.

Apple Inc.

Sign in with Apple, on Android as well as on iPhone; the Apple Push Notification service, which is the last step of every notification delivered to an iPhone; App Attest, for device attestation; and the App Store, for purchases.

RevenueCat, Inc.

On iPhone and on Android builds configured for subscriptions, RevenueCat processes your Iris account identifier, purchase and receipt history, subscription status and device information from its software. This includes guest identifiers, so a purchase can be attached before optional registration. On iPhone its software also collects Apple’s advertising-attribution token. Billing availability depends on the installed build and store configuration; an unavailable offer does not mean that all subscription processing is inactive.

GitHub, Inc.

Hosts the page you are reading. It therefore sees your IP address and browser details when you open it, as any web host would. It receives nothing else and has no access to your Iris account.

Transfer out of your country

All processing takes place in the United States. Where you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, these transfers are made under the data processing agreements we hold with each company above, which incorporate the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses.

9Purchases

The tracker and full-quality local habit photographs are free. Ongoing Iris coaching is an optional subscription where offered. Your installed app and the native App Store or Google Play purchase sheet show the available monthly or annual plan, localized price, billing period, renewal conditions and any trial. A seven-day trial is available only when the store says you are eligible; do not assume a previous subscriber qualifies. The store’s displayed terms apply before you confirm. A purchase is not required to continue tracking.

Payment is handled by Apple or Google. To restore, use Restore purchases in Iris with the store account that bought the subscription and the correct Iris account. If verification is pending or access is missing, contact support before buying again. Manage or cancel the subscription with the store that billed you. Deleting Iris, deleting an Iris account, or withdrawing AI permission does not cancel a store subscription. Cancel before the trial or renewal deadline shown by that store to avoid the next charge.

10What the models are trained on

We have not permitted any of the companies in section 8 to train models on what you send to Iris, and each of them states that it does not:

  • OpenAI does not use data submitted through its API to train its models, and keeps abuse-monitoring logs for up to thirty days.
  • Anthropic does not use commercial API inputs or outputs to train its models by default, and states that it deletes them from its systems within thirty days by default.
  • Google’s Gemini API, on the paid service we use, does not use prompts or responses to improve Google’s products; Google logs them for a limited period only, in order to detect breaches of its prohibited-use policy and to meet legal obligations.
  • Google Cloud Text-to-Speech runs under the Google Cloud terms, which are separate from the above, and under which customer data is not used to train Google’s models.

These are the providers’ own commitments under the paid business terms on which we hold our accounts. They are not guarantees we are in a position to give on another company’s behalf; each provider’s current terms govern. If that position changes, this section changes with it.

13How long we keep it

Your profile, habits, weekly history, goals, milestones and archived conversations are kept until you delete your account. There is no automatic expiry, and the application does not let you read archived conversations back.

Two things expire on their own: competition photographs are deleted when the round is decided and in no case kept beyond eight days; the cached speech audio described in section 7 is deleted after thirty days. The record of a device is deleted when you sign out of it.

Where in-app reporting is available, a report contains your account identifier, the selected content, its reference, your reason and any note you choose to send. Only the developer and authorised moderators can read the private report. Selected reported content and moderation records are retained for up to 90 days, unless applicable law requires otherwise. Backups used to restore hidden content expire after 90 days. Account deletion removes reports and backups tied to your account under the account-deletion policy.

14Deleting your account

In the app, open the menu, your profile and Delete account. You may need to sign in again to confirm ownership or revoke Apple access. Deletion is permanent once completed. If the operation reports a failure, contact us so we can check the remaining cleanup.

You can request deletion even if you have uninstalled Iris or cannot open it. Email iris26app@gmail.com with the subject “Delete my Iris account”. Include your Iris username or the sign-in email and whether you used Apple or Google. If you used Hide My Email, include the Apple relay address if known. For a guest, include the account identifier if you have it; tell us if you do not. You do not need to reinstall the app to make a request.

We verify ownership before deleting anyone’s data, normally by replying to the address associated with the account. We never ask for your password, verification codes, payment-card details or a payment. If a guest account has no recoverable identifier or linked sign-in, we may be unable to identify it safely; we will explain what can be verified. We respond within ten working days and explain the verification and completion steps.

Deletion removes the sign-in identity, profile, habits, history, goals, milestones, conversations, settings, device records, onboarding diagnostics, queued coaching jobs, social connections, uploaded avatar, competition scores and shared photographs associated with the account. Competitions created by you are removed; other competitions can continue without you. In-app deletion also clears Iris photographs on that device. An email request cannot remotely erase files on an offline device.

Some information is outside that deletion: copies in your gallery or device backups; files on another device until that device clears them; already-decided competition results; text-addressed speech caches that expire after 30 days; and security logs under the hosting provider’s retention. Store transaction records follow Apple’s or Google’s retention. RevenueCat records are handled separately: ask us to coordinate the deletion request. Support correspondence is kept as needed to resolve and document the request, and applicable legal retention may still apply. Competition photo copies expire when the round is decided, with an eight-day storage expiry backstop.

Account deletion and subscription cancellation are separate. Cancel an active subscription with Apple or Google Play; uninstalling or deleting your account does not stop renewal. You may request account deletion without reinstalling or buying anything.
